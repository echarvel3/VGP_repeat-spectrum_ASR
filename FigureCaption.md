VGP genomes dramatically increase the repetitiveness of the genomes. We compare a subset of 257 VGP genomes against corresponding existing whole-genome assemblies, including all VGP assemblies with at least one comparable existing assembly. We calculated the repeat spectrum for k-mers (k=31), measuring how many k-mers appear once, twice, etc. We report the percentage of the genome made up of k-mers that appear only once **(A, left)**, the HCRM measure of highly repeated k-mers **(A, middle)**, and genome length **(A, right)**. HCRM (high-count repeat per million) is defined as the average count per million base-pairs of the 10 most repetitive k-mers. Triangles represent the results of ancestral state reconstruction for each metric. Genome length increases modestly while repetitiveness increases dramatically, showing that missing parts of the genome were mostly repetitive regions. Inferred ancestral level of repeats is under-estimated with existing assemblies. **B)** For each frequency of k-mers, we show what percentage of the genome was made up of k-mers with that frequency in existing assemblies (arrow tail) and in new VGP assemblies (arrow head), breaking by lineage and showing the mean for each lineage (log-scale). The upward arrows (prominent for birds and cartilaginous fish) for frequencies above 1 show an increase in representativeness, especially at higher frequencies. **C)** The ancestral state reconstruction for the VGP assemblies for the three metrics shown in (A). Early vertebrates are inferred to have had highly repetitive genomes, with dramatic reductions in birds and, to a lesser extent, in mammals.
